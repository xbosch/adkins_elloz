The Naked World

Six poems on weightlessness, order, and a body standing in plain light.

Lightness

The earth breathes low
under an unhurried light.
Stone, grass, shadow:
a suspended balance.

The river, slow, moves on
through barefoot bends.
The water holds no shape,
it becomes the path.

At the banks, white lilies
drink the morning's clarity;
further off, upright cypresses
do not wait for the wind.

Open sea,
a circle of shores.
Waves come and go,
undoing themselves each instant.

Faceless wind
moves leaves, clouds, dust,
and vanishes without a name.

The rain falls straight:
bubbles on the water.
Every mark dissolves
into a wider silence.

Everything slides.
Everything yields.
Nothing stays still enough
to become weight.

In open calm,
without center or edge,
the earth turns slowly
and the weight goes unfelt.

Perfection

The eye empties.
Autumn light on the stone,
with no shadow of the one who looks.

The tree holds up the sky.
No gesture, no plea:
exact structure,
the knot that asks nothing.

The water descends slowly,
undoing its own shape
to be only a path.

A bird cuts the air.
The line closes behind the wing.
There is no memory of the flight left,
no reason to return.

Pure silence of basalt.
The earth turns in the void
and the weight does not ache:
it is the order itself.

Clarity

The weight lifts
without leaving a void,
like fog when it rises.

Joy and sadness
dissolve at the edges,
and time stops holding on.

Voices, footsteps, thoughts
cross the air
without staying.

A body remains standing
inside the morning's clarity.
The light enters the eyes
in silence.

What weighed
is now presence.

Anchor

Sitting on the stone bench,
the afternoon falls exact
in a thin air.

Ahead, the tree lifts its trunk
without gesture or will,
rooted in an old ground
that has not been touched.

No leaf asks
for anything time could give;
it only holds the light
without having to wait.

The world passes at the edge,
brief, scattered, uncertain;
the shadow at the foot of the trunk
has not given up an inch.

The bench receives the light
without holding onto it;
the shadow falls from the tree
without the tree claiming it.

And in this simple being,
with no before or after,
the instant where nothing is lost
is kept in the motionless tree.

The tree does not wait for the wind
or for the sun to release it:
it stays where it is, exact.

Ruin

The sky, left open too long,
a dulled green at the edges,
black shadow nailed
under a wall that does not yield.

The day falls straight
with its bitter limits,
like a silent law
already fully served.

Nothing turns aside.
The light does not console.
The air weighs, motionless,
over the mute earth.

No wing crosses it,
no voice wants to move it.
Everything stays where it fell,
with a peace that burns.

Not a single blade dares
to undo the stillness,
and the world turns just the same,
exact, empty,
forever unchanged.

The afternoon comes down slowly,
with no promise and no shadow,
like a gesture erasing itself
before it was born.

The stones keep the hour
not knowing whose it was,
and time passes, obedient,
leaving no footprint.

And under this clean order
that never trembles,
something absent
has learned not to return.

Yellow

The light lodges itself at a slant,
electric yellow bores into the stone,
climbs the edges, vibrates in the air:
the day, tensed
into an angle with no return.

The ground tilts underfoot,
refuses to be flat, twists;
planes slide, horizonless slopes
gather speed without falling.

Dust no longer floats: it churns,
files the air at half height,
scrapes the throat, sinks into the tongue,
a dry invasion the body cannot expel.

The crack runs while it is still a crack,
opens, forks,
advances mute;
stone gives way
without having fallen.

Shadows flee their objects,
break loose, come undone,
off their axis;
the eye chases, uselessly,
outlines that no longer match.

No silence: the creak of weight,
the violent misalignment of everything holding on,
the world spills into a second too long
where form wavers,

and the yellow light
finds no shadow left.
