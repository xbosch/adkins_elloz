High Seas

Four poems: a calm harbor, a departure, a sudden gust, a held course.

Read alongside Rising Form

Spring Sun

The sun warms the skin.
The wind, gentle, calms the body.
It does not push. It surrounds.
The sea horizon,
a still line.
The harbor does not matter.
The wood does not matter.
Everything holds itself up.
Lightness over the sea.
Tissue paper over the calm.
Time stretches out in the shadows.
The landscape holds.

At Dawn

The harbor is still.
The moorings hang slack.
The wood rests at the dock.
Outside,
the sea can turn.
The wind comes down from the north.
The channel mouth stands open.
The boat remembers.
Old crossings.
Long nights.
The wood hesitates.
The hand grips tighter.
The sea does not always forgive.
We could stay.
The dock is firm.
The land is certain.
But promises have been made.
The moorings fall.
The bow seeks the sea.
The harbor falls behind.

Gust

The sea changes.
Everything is still.
The sail hangs.
The water is smooth.
Now the wind blows.
Just once.
The sail fills and drops.
The sea is still flat.
The wood shudders.
Everything trembles.

Course

Sail trimmed.
Bow to the wind.
Course held firm.
The hull cuts the water.
The waves do not impose themselves.
The sea opens.
The hand holds the helm.
The course does not give.
The horizon, far off.
The sea reveals itself,
untamed
and sailed.
